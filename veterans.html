Veterans’ Story Hour

You will no doubt have heard of Drag Queen Story Hour, where colourful drag artists visit schools and libraries to tell stories to young and older children. I would like to harness some of that energy and design to bring Armed Forces veterans instead — men and women who have borne the weight of service — into classrooms, youth centres and libraries to recount lived stories of bravery, honour and quiet pride.

Life is hard. It is meant to be so. The difficulty is not an aberration but the very condition that confers worth upon achievement. Success lightly won is success lightly valued; that which is wrested from adversity carries a deeper savour. Failure, too, will come — sometimes repeatedly. This is neither tragedy nor indictment.

The only true failure is the abandonment of the struggle itself. To persist, to rise again, to refuse the counsel of despair: these are the lessons that endure, and they are best learned not from theory but from those who have practised them under fire or in the long, unglamorous grind of duty.

The narratives we propose are those of patriotism and civic pride — stories drawn from every background, every race, yet all of them British and all of them unashamed of that identity. In a country growing ever more multiracial, the shared language of belonging becomes more, not less, essential. Without it, the mosaic risks becoming a mere heap of fragments. Civic pride and patriotism are not relics of a vanished age; they are the mortar that still binds.

We require fresh channels through which these living testimonies may reach the young — channels that speak with the authority of experience rather than the rhetoric of ideology. It is with that conviction that I invite you to the inaugural pilot of this modest venture, to be held at the public house I keep in Oxfordshire. There, over tea and a bacon roll in unpretentious surroundings, we shall begin.

Yours sincerely,
Rory Hanrahan
