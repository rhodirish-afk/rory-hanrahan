I Stand With British Jews

A quiet campaign of solidarity.

On Yom Kippur 20–21 September 2026 — the holiest day in the Jewish calendar — I am asking ordinary British people to wear a simple badge bearing the Union Flag and the words “I Stand With British Jews”.

No marches.
No rallies.
No foreign-policy slogans.

Just a small, visible act of neighbourly decency.

10 pence from every badge sold goes directly to AJEX – The Jewish Military Association, supporting Jewish veterans, serving personnel and their families.

Why this day

Yom Kippur (the Day of Atonement) is the most solemn day in the Jewish year. It is a time of reflection, fasting and prayer. Choosing this day for a quiet show of solidarity is deliberate: it is not about politics or protest. It is about standing with our Jewish neighbours on the day that means most to them.

British Jews are our neighbours. They deserve to feel safe and supported in their own country. A simple badge is one small way to say so.

AJEX – The Jewish Military Association

AJEX (full name: AJEX – The Jewish Military Association) is the organisation that represents Jewish men and women who have served, or are still serving, in the British Armed Forces.

Its roots go back to the years after the First World War. Around 50,000 British Jews served in that conflict. In 1921 a group of Jewish veterans laid a wreath at the Cenotaph — a public statement that Jewish service was British service. In 1929, amid rising antisemitism, the Jewish Ex-Servicemen’s Legion was formed. It became the Association of Jewish Ex-Servicemen and Women in 1936 and is now known as AJEX – The Jewish Military Association.

More than 70,000 British Jews served in the Second World War. AJEX has three core purposes that have remained consistent for nearly a century:

  • Remembrance — ensuring the Jewish contribution to Britain’s armed forces is never forgotten. The annual AJEX Remembrance Parade and Ceremony at the Cenotaph on Whitehall is one of the most important events in the communal calendar.
  • Welfare — using the stories of Jewish service to teach British values, counter the old lie that “Jews did not fight”, and push back against contemporary hatred.
  • Education and the fight against antisemitism — using the stories of Jewish service to teach British values, counter the old lie that “Jews did not fight”, and push back against contemporary hatred.

AJEX also works closely with currently serving Jewish personnel through the Armed Forces Jewish Community and maintains the Jewish Military Museum and the Record of Honour.
