Campaign

I Stand With British Jews

A quiet campaign of solidarity. Badges, not marches.

British Jews are our neighbours. They should be able to live openly and safely in the country they have served for generations. That is no longer guaranteed. The weather has turned.

At its conference the Green Party voted, by 1,022 to 813, that Zionism is racism and that the party should treat it as such. Members also rejected a safeguard for Jews who belong to a synagogue that prays for the State of Israel. A major British political party has now decided that the ordinary Jewish belief in a Jewish homeland is a form of racism. That is not a debate about settlements or Gaza. It is a decision that most British Jews, by definition, hold a racist belief. The company that slogan keeps is not new. It was the territory of fascists and communists. It is now party policy.

At the same time, something colder has arrived on British streets. MI5 has confirmed what Jewish communities already knew: Iran is subcontracting attacks on Jewish targets through local criminals and disposable young men. Pay to slay. Pay-as-you-go terrorism. Arson attacks on Hatzola ambulances, synagogues, Jewish charities and businesses in London. A claimed campaign by a front group tied to the IRGC. Two Iranian nationals charged over a plot targeting Jews in the Manchester area. More than fifty arrests linked to Iranian operations in the last year. The men arrested near RAF Fairford were released on bail. The pattern is simple and vicious: recruit online, pay in crypto, demand video proof, escalate the jobs, keep the sponsor invisible. It works precisely because the foot-soldiers are cheap and replaceable.

This is not the ordinary friction of a diverse country. It is the industrial preparation of the ground so that attacking Jews once again looks, to enough people, like resistance rather than crime. Jewish schools and synagogues already run under security that would have been unthinkable a generation ago. Parents think twice before sending children to youth clubs. Families debate whether Britain is still a safe place to raise Jewish children. Some will leave. Britain loses either way.

None of this is normal, and none of us should get used to it.

British Jews have served this country in numbers far higher than their share of the population. Around 50,000 in the First World War, more than 60,000 in the Second. Victoria Cross recipients, the Jewish Brigade, ordinary men and women in every service. AJEX still looks after those who served and those who still serve.

I am asking ordinary British people — English, Scottish, Welsh, Northern Irish, of any background — to wear a simple badge with the Union Flag and the words I Stand With British Jews. Not on one day of the year. Every day. On the bus, in the pub, at the school gate, in the office.

No marches. No rallies. No foreign-policy slogans. Just a small, visible act of neighbourly decency that says: British Jews still have friends in Britain. They still belong here. They are not the “other.”

Order a badge. Wear it. Speak up when you hear the old hatred in new language — at the bar, at work, in the family group chat. Use Jewish businesses. Ask how your Jewish neighbours are, and mean it. Share the campaign so others can do the same.

This is not about Israel. It is about whether Britain remains a country in which a family that has lived here for centuries can get on a bus in Golders Green, or walk to shul, or send a child to school, without calculating the risk. We have been here before. We know where it leads when decent people decide it is someone else’s problem.

Wear the badge. Stand with them.
