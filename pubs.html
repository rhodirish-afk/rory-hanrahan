The pubs

The Vine Inn & the rest

I manage three village pubs in Oxfordshire. The one I spend most of my time in is The Vine Inn in Cumnor — a proper old place with a beer garden, hand pumps, and the daily arithmetic of keeping real ale in good condition when trade is unpredictable.

I was born above a pub my family has owned since 1721. Twenty-odd years of multi-site publican life later, the problems are familiar: rising costs, thin margins, and the quiet, grinding difficulty of keeping cask ale fresh when the garden is half-empty on a Tuesday.

Still pouring after 35 days

A real-world trial of Freshkask at The Vine Inn — and why it matters for the future of cask ale. Read the full piece →

In early June I wrote in The Spectator that the World Cup would not save England’s pubs. Nowhere is that difficulty sharper than with cask. Once a traditional cask is tapped, most quieter pubs have a window of two to five days at best.

On 24 June 2026 we put two Jennings beers on in Freshkask vessels: Back Yam and Cocker Hoop. A heatwave arrived and the cellar cooling failed. We improvised with ice packs, wet towels and fans. At fourteen days both beers were still clear, bright and true to style. One vessel was still pouring in superb condition on 29 July — thirty-five days after it first went on.
